Socks Knitting Machines Explained: Cylinder Size, Needle Gauge & How They Shape Your Order

📅 May 10, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read 🏷️ Manufacturing

When you order custom socks, the machine that makes them quietly decides your quality ceiling, pattern options, and minimum order quantity. Most buyers never see the knitting floor—but understanding three numbers will make you a far sharper negotiator.

1. Cylinder Diameter: The Size of the Sock

The cylinder is the circular bed of needles that forms the sock tube. Its diameter (measured in inches) determines the finished size:

  • 3.5"–4" cylinders: kids and ankle socks
  • 4"–4.5" cylinders: women's and standard crew socks
  • 5"–6" cylinders: men's, compression, and thermal socks

A factory with a wide range of cylinder sizes can serve more product categories without outsourcing—keeping your lead time and cost under control.

2. Needle Gauge: The Detail Level

Gauge = needles per inch around the cylinder. Higher gauge means finer, denser, more detailed fabric.

  • 96–144N (coarse): thick terry, boot and work socks
  • 168–200N (mid): everyday crew and athletic socks
  • 200N+ (fine): dress socks, sheer and fashion jacquard
📊 Entrol Rongxing: 800 machines spanning 96N to 220N, covering coarse needle, double-cylinder jacquard and fine dress sock production in-house.

3. Single vs. Double Cylinder

This is the decision that affects your pattern richness most:

  • Single cylinder: faster, cheaper, good for plain and simple patterned socks.
  • Double cylinder: enables true 3D jacquard, intarsia logos and complex knit structures—the choice for branded and premium socks.

4. How Machines Drive Your MOQ

Machine setup is the hidden cost behind MOQ. Each style change requires re-mounting cylinders and dials. A factory with more machines can dedicate units to your style and accept a lower MOQ; one running at full capacity will push MOQs up.

A realistic MOQ of 3,000 pairs per design reflects a factory confident it can slot your order without displacing large retail runs.

5. Questions to Ask Your Supplier

  1. What cylinder size range do you operate?
  2. Which gauges are available for my target fabric weight?
  3. Are double-cylinder jacquard machines in-house or subcontracted?
  4. What is the changeover time between designs?
