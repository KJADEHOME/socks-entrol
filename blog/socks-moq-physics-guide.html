Sock MOQ Is Physics, Not Policy: 3 Hard Floors and How to Lower Yours

📅 September 25, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 💰 Buyer Planning

"Can you do 500 pairs instead of 1,000?" We get asked this almost every week. And the honest answer is: sometimes yes — but not because we suddenly became flexible.

That 1,000 isn't a sales policy. It's three physical floors stacked on top of each other. Once you see where each floor comes from, the conversation changes from "can you bend?" to "which floor are we actually hitting?"

1. The Dye Bath

A dye vessel has a minimum liquor ratio. Below roughly 30–50 kg per colour, the bath can't circulate evenly and you get shade drift inside a single order. A men's sock weighs 45–60 g. Do the maths: one colourway is already 600–1,100 pairs before the colour is even trustworthy. Six colourways isn't six times the socks — it's six separate baths.

2. The Machine Setup

Changing gauge or pattern means re-threading 168–200 needles and running the first 30–50 pairs to waste until tension stabilises. That setup costs the same whether we then knit 300 pairs or 30,000. At 300 pairs, it lands on every single pair you buy.

3. The Yarn Lot

Spinners sell in lots, not in the exact kilos you need. Open a lot for 300 pairs and the rest of that yarn sits in our warehouse as your private dead stock — and your next reorder may not be dye-lot matchable to it.

MOQ is the smallest run where all three floors are still efficient. Lower than that and you're not "getting a deal" — you're paying for waste you can't see on the quote.

5 Ways to Launch a Smaller First Run (Without a Surcharge)

So when a buyer says "just meet me at 500," here's what I actually propose instead:

  1. Cut colourways, not pairs — 3 × 500 beats 6 × 250 every time.
  2. Use our stock-service shades — no dye-lot minimum at all.
  3. Piece-dye instead of yarn-dye for the first run.
  4. Start with two size blocks, not a full 35–46 grid.
  5. Ask for TWO quotes: unit price at MOQ, and at your real quantity — then decide whether the premium is worth the cash you're freeing up.
Real result: A start-up took options 1 and 4 with us recently and launched at roughly 40% of our standard MOQ without paying a cent of surcharge. Same sock. Fewer variables. Same margin for both of us.

None of these require us to "bend the rules." They simply move the run onto a configuration where the three floors stop biting.
