Socks MOQ & Pricing Demystified: What Really Drives Your Unit Cost

📅 June 15, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read 💰 Pricing

"Why is this sock $0.45 and that one $1.20?" It is the question every buyer asks. The answer is rarely yarn alone. Here are the five levers that move your quote.

1. Order Volume (The Biggest Lever)

Sock production has heavy fixed setup cost per design. Spreading it across more pairs drops the unit price fast.

  • 3,000 pairs/design: highest unit cost, lowest commitment
  • 10,000+ pairs/design: meaningful discount
  • 50,000+ pairs/design:接近最优成本

2. Yarn Composition & Certification

Combed cotton costs more than carded. Recycled (GRS) and organic yarns carry certification premiums—but unlock EU/US retail shelves.

💰 Rule of thumb: Every upgrade in yarn grade or certification adds 5–20% to material cost, but often 2–3x the retail margin.

3. Gauge & Fabric Weight

Finer gauges (200N+) and thicker terry loops use more yarn and machine time. A plush thermal sock will cost more than a thin ankle sock of the same size.

4. Pattern Complexity

Plain knit is cheapest. Double-cylinder jacquard with multiple colors and logo placement adds programming and inspection time. Functional zones (cushion, arch support) add steps.

5. Packaging & Labeling

Single polybag vs. hang-tag + display box vs. RFID-labeled retail pack—packaging can be 5–15% of landed cost and affects your shelf readiness.

A transparent quote breaks cost into yarn, knitting, finishing, and packing. If a supplier can't, that is a red flag.

How to Get the Best Price

  1. Consolidate colors into fewer designs to raise per-design volume.
  2. Decide certification needs early—retrofitting is expensive.
  3. Ask for an itemized quote and compare like-for-like.
