Socks Quality Standards & Compliance: AQL, OEKO-TEX & What Retailers Require

📅 June 28, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✂️ Quality

Quality is not just "it looks fine." For B2B and retail socks, it means translating the buyer's specification into measurable checkpoints, an approved reference and an agreed inspection plan. Certificates support due diligence, but they do not replace product-level control.

1. AQL: The Inspection Language

Acceptance Quality Limit (AQL) is a sampling approach used to decide whether a production lot meets an agreed acceptance standard. The inspection level, sample size, defect classes and limits should be specified by the buyer or agreed before production.

  • Critical defects: issues that may create a safety or regulatory risk
  • Major defects: failures that affect use, saleability or the approved specification
  • Minor defects: workmanship or appearance differences that do not normally prevent use

Typical sock checks cover holes, dropped stitches, incorrect sizing, poor toe seams, visible yarn faults, shade differences, misplaced artwork, labeling and assortment. The buyer should define how each issue is classified for the order.

2. Material Safety: OEKO-TEX 100

STANDARD 100 by OEKO-TEX is a testing and certification system for textiles tested for harmful substances. Buyers should request the current certificate, check the certificate number and confirm that its product scope covers the proposed goods.

Rongxing documentation: The factory can provide current OEKO-TEX, BSCI and Sedex documentation for buyer review. Applicability and scope should be confirmed for the specific program.

3. Social & Environmental Compliance

Many retailers also review workplace and supply-chain practices. Different programs ask for different platforms, audit protocols and validity periods:

  • amfori BSCI: a social-audit framework used in responsible sourcing programs
  • Sedex: a platform used to share responsible-sourcing information and audit data
  • Buyer-specific requirements: retailer manuals may add testing, labeling or audit conditions

4. Our 6-Stage QC in Practice

  1. Yarn incoming inspection
  2. In-line knitting inspection
  3. Linking / toe inspection
  4. Boarding / shape inspection
  5. Final AQL random inspection
  6. Pre-shipment inspection

For a closer view of these checkpoints, see our sock quality inspection service and six-stage quality-control guide.

5. What to Request From Any Supplier

  • Current certificate copies with scope and validity that match the intended program
  • An approved sample and written product specification
  • The inspection level, defect classification and acceptance limits
  • Testing, labeling, packaging and carton-mark requirements for the destination market
  • A documented process for reporting and resolving non-conformities

6. Build the Quality Plan Before Sampling

Send the target size chart, fiber composition, construction notes, color references, artwork position, packaging and required tests with the initial inquiry. During sampling, record any approved deviations and convert the final sample into a measurable bulk standard. If a third-party inspection is required, confirm who appoints the inspector and which edition of the buyer manual applies.
